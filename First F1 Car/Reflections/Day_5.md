## 💭 Reflection
After taking a break from this project to recharge and enjoy my vacation, I was excited to return and continue developing the Formula One car model. Today's session felt noticeably different from the earlier stages of the project. Instead of struggling to understand SolidWorks features or constantly troubleshooting basic modeling issues, I was able to `approach each component with much more confidence and a clearer understanding` of how different features interact with one another.

The session `started smoothly` with the addition of the nose winglets. Thanks to the experience I gained from previous days with separate bodies, fillets, and reference geometry, creating these aerodynamic elements was relatively straightforward. I was able to `design, refine, and integrate them into the chassis without major difficulties`. The same applied to the side mirrors, which mainly involved fundamental sketching and extrusion techniques. These tasks may have seemed simple compared to some of the challenges I faced earlier, but they highlighted something important: `my improvement was not only measured by learning new features, but also by how naturally I could apply existing knowledge`. Features and concepts that once required significant thought were now becoming part of my normal workflow.

I then moved on to refining the rear wing. After making several extrude cuts and applying fillets to improve the geometry, the rear wing was finalized. This process was relatively smooth, allowing me to focus more on design accuracy and aesthetics rather than troubleshooting.

The `most challenging part` of today's session was `creating the sidepod louvers`. The tutorial's original approach did not fully work with my model, although I was able to replicate most of the steps. The tutorial created the sidepod louver structure by first making an extrude-cut block, creating a vertical face sketch, and then using a 3D sketch of a horizontal and perpendicular guide line. The guideline was then used with the Sweep feature to create the louver geometry, followed by a Linear Pattern to duplicate the elements. The issue occurred when I attempted to `create the horizontal and perpendicular 3D sketch`. For some reason, SolidWorks would not allow me to create the same perpendicular relation that was shown in the tutorial. After multiple attempts to replicate the exact process, I decided that continuing to force the same approach was inefficient. Instead, I used my `understanding of reference geometry to develop an alternative solution`. I created a new reference plane that intersected the vertical face sketch and positioned it perpendicular to the side of the louver block. This allowed me to create a sketch line that was both horizontal and perpendicular to the geometry, achieving `the same result as the tutorial method`. This was one of the most satisfying moments of today's session because I was able to `overcome the problem quickly using my own understanding of SolidWorks` rather than relying on the tutorial. Earlier in the project, I often searched for solutions whenever I encountered unfamiliar errors. Today, I was able to `analyze the problem, identify what the model needed, and create my own solution`. This demonstrated `a significant improvement in both my technical knowledge and problem-solving ability`.

Another minor challenge appeared when `integrating the new components into the main chassis`. One of the nose winglets was not combining with the body as expected. After investigating the issue, I realized that the fillet geometry on the winglet was preventing the bodies from merging correctly. Instead of forcing the combination feature, I `reviewed the feature history, checked the relationships connected to the fillet, and carefully modified the feature before proceeding`. This was a particularly meaningful moment because it `directly contrasted with the mistake I made during Day 4`. Previously, I deleted a critical fillet without considering its dependencies, causing major errors throughout the model. This time, I approached the modification more carefully by `understanding the feature's role before making changes`. It showed `a greater level of maturity and awareness when working with complex CAD models`.

Overall, today's session was one of the smoothest I've experienced so far. However, the reason it felt easier was not because the components themselves were simple but because `my understanding of SolidWorks has improved significantly`. Concepts such as reference geometry, separate bodies, fillets, and feature relationships are becoming more intuitive, allowing me to `focus more on designing rather than understanding and experiencing the software`. Seeing the car gradually transform from a collection of basic shapes into a detailed Formula One machine has been incredibly rewarding. More importantly, this project has shown me that `engineering growth comes from repeatedly facing challenges, learning from mistakes, and building confidence through experience`.
